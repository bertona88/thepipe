# Integrated mechanisms and fluidic possibilities

**Status: exploration.** Printing links, elastic elements and cavities together allows functions that would otherwise require separate tiny components.

## Channels as structure

A channel can carry vacuum or liquid through a rigid link to a fingertip. That directly follows the user's proposal. 2PP microfluidic integration has been demonstrated in other devices; the relevant precedent does not establish a leak-free lumen through a moving flexure. See [sources](../06-reference/sources-and-glossary.md).

The geometric design should expose clean-out paths and avoid making the narrowest lumen the least inspectable part of the mechanism. A replaceable distal fluidic tip may be easier to operate than a permanently integrated long passage.

## Pressure-driven motion

A cavity, diaphragm or compliant wall could convert external pressure into jaw or wrist motion. This is an extension of the fluid-channel idea, not a selected replacement for tendons. It could reduce fibre routing for a specialised tool while adding pressure response, leakage and material deformation to the control problem.

## Passive valves and flow features

Printed restrictions, capillary stops or compliant valve-like features could shape fluid delivery. Small channels create opportunities for passive behaviour, but also make contamination and process variation influential. A geometrically present valve is not evidence of a repeatable cracking pressure or seal.

## Latches and bistability

A compliant latch could hold a tool or fixture state without continuous actuation. A bistable jaw might use energy only to switch states. The tradeoff is snap-through and release energy near delicate parts. Observability of the actual latched state is as important as the switching command.

## Hierarchical compliance

The same material could make a stiff support, a compliant joint and a more compliant contact feature through geometry. This could give an arm spatially tailored stiffness without multiple resins. It also creates several possible deformation modes that must be distinguished in the model or measurement.

The design opportunity is functional integration with accessible evidence. Combining every function in one print is not intrinsically better than a small number of understandable, replaceable pieces.
