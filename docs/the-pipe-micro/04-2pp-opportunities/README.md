# What adopting 2PP unlocks

This section preserves the ambitious side of the discussion. The common enabler is the ability to put intricate geometry exactly where mechanical, optical or surface behaviour is needed. These are possibilities for the platform, not a feature list required for its first embodiment.

| Opportunity | Geometric freedom being used | Detailed document |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Switchable dry attachment | Inclined, compliant or hierarchical contact elements | [Directional adhesion](directional-adhesion.md) |
| Surface-tension tools | Contact topology, meniscus geometry and wicking paths | [Wet tools and capillary transfer](wet-tools-and-capillary-transfer.md) |
| Cooperative fixtures and parts | Lead-ins, clips, handling tabs and locating features | [Passive fixtures and part design](passive-fixtures-and-part-design.md) |
| Sensing without distal electronics | Calibrated flexure deformation and printed optical references | [Optical and mechanical sensing](optical-and-mechanical-sensing.md) |
| Integrated mechanical functions | Channels, passive valves, latches and compliant mechanisms | [Integrated and fluidic mechanisms](integrated-and-fluidic-mechanisms.md) |
| A machine that helps build its successors | Small keyed assembly interfaces and temporary handling structures | [Assembling the next arms](assembling-the-next-arms.md) |

The first five grow directly from ideas in the conversation, with some elaboration of their mechanisms. The last develops the user's explicit idea that an initial single-material machine could assemble later arms from different materials.

Geometry-first does not mean geometry-only forever. Fluorination, dissipative layers and material-specific surfaces may eventually solve a measured problem. They remain optional extensions rather than assumptions hidden in the first native-resin concept.
