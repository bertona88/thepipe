# Assembling the next arms

**Status: exploration explicitly proposed by the user.** Once an initial single-material manipulator can handle small components reliably, it may help assemble later arms and tools made from different materials.

## The bootstrapping idea

The first arm need not already solve multimaterial fabrication. It can manipulate separately produced components: a stiffer link, a compliant insert, a specialised contact pad, a fibre, a channel section or a tool cartridge. Printed handling interfaces and fixtures can make those components accessible to the initial machine.

This is machine-assisted assembly of successors. It does not imply autonomous manufacture of motors, cameras, resins or every component of the cell.

## What 2PP adds to assembly

Parts can carry alignment keys, sockets, snap features, tendon-routing eyes, fiducials and temporary handling tabs. The assembly fixture can constrain intermediate structures while several arms join or tension them. The receiving geometry can turn a difficult free-space alignment into a guided operation.

## Joining remains a physical question

Candidate joining methods include mechanical capture, compliant clips, interference features and later adhesive or other bonding methods. The appropriate choice depends on loads, contamination, optical access and reversibility. Adhesive curing or welding is not assumed to be available merely because parts can be placed together.

Tendon installation may be particularly demanding: threading, anchoring, setting preload and verifying the resulting motion are different operations. A larger temporary tendon eye or a staged anchor can help, but the complete assembly path must exist.

## Useful later capabilities

The machine might replace a worn fingertip, fit a special contact material, assemble a longer arm from several pieces, add a fluidic tool or construct fixtures for new tasks. These are concrete extensions of microassembly skill, with increasing complexity determined by what the first system can actually hold and release.

## Evidence of success

The assembled successor must do something useful under observation: maintain its joint range, carry intended tendon loads, hold a tool and execute a bounded operation. A visually completed arm is not sufficient if its bonds, preload or contact surfaces have not been exercised.

The single-material first structure is therefore an enabling starting point, not a permanent restriction on what The Pipe can build.
