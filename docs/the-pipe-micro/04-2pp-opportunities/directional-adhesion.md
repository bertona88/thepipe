# Directional dry adhesion and gecko-like tools

**Status: exploration raised in the discussion.** The attractive idea is a contact that holds well under one loading direction and detaches under another.

## How it could work

Inclined fibrils, ribs or compliant contact elements can change their contact area and load sharing as shear direction changes. A tool could engage through a deliberate tangential motion, carry under a favourable load direction and release by changing angle or initiating peel. The arm motion and pad geometry would be designed together.

The geometry can be simpler than a biological gecko foot because both the workpiece and the tool may be designed. A small handling tab with a known surface is an easier first interface than arbitrary rough objects.

## Why it is distinct from anti-adhesion

A sparse stiff mesa aims to limit intimate contact. A compliant fibrillar pad may deliberately increase conformity and distribute load. Both are microtextures, but they can produce opposite behaviour. A coupon family should include both branches without interpreting all texture as a route to lower adhesion.

## What is unlocked

A single-sided pickup could expose more of a part than opposed jaws. Directional holding may allow a small motion to switch attachment state. A receiver pad could engage in its favourable direction while the donor peels away. Tool and destination could have deliberately different contact responses using the same structural resin.

## What could defeat it

The selected resin may not provide the required combination of compliance and durability. Fibrils can collapse, stick to each other during processing, wear, trap contaminants or damage the object. Humidity and charge may dominate the intended directional effect. Excessive engagement shear could move or scratch a fragile part before it is securely acquired.

## Evidence that would make it credible

Compare holding and release as a function of loading direction, preload, speed and cycle count. Observe the part's displacement during engagement as well as detachment. An attractive result would be a repeatable directional operating window with adequate absolute retention and small release error.

No gecko-like performance is claimed for the first resin. The idea remains valuable precisely because a geometry family can test whether that useful window exists.
