# Passive fixtures and parts designed for assembly

**Status: exploration extending the discussion's cooperative-release and printed-surface ideas.** The destination can contribute geometry and retention instead of serving as a passive point in space.

## A printed fixture as another hand

Three or more strategically placed locating contacts can define a repeatable seat for a suitable component. A compliant clip can retain it after tool withdrawal. A lead-in can convert a bounded alignment error into a guided motion. Relief regions can keep unwanted broad surfaces apart while contact occurs only at intended supports.

The exact constraint scheme depends on the part. Overconstraint, high insertion load and unintended adhesion can defeat an apparently self-aligning seat. Flexures in the fixture can relieve small errors while leaving selected directions well defined.

## Design the part's handling interface

When parts are printable, they can include handling tabs, gripping flats, fiducial regions, temporary supports or orientation keys. A gear's functional tooth and bearing surfaces need not also be its handling surfaces. A temporary feature may allow secure manipulation without contaminating a critical face.

Removal of temporary supports is itself an operation: it can release stored energy, generate debris or impose a load on the completed assembly. A feature is valuable only if its complete lifecycle is manageable.

## Contact paths rather than sticky surprises

The fixture can deliberately provide a receiving interface that remains engaged while the donor peels or vents. Sparse supports may minimise unwanted adhesion elsewhere. A geometrically keyed seat can reduce dependence on the native surface's friction.

## Why it changes the robot

Useful fixture geometry can reduce the orientation or force-control burden on a distal arm. Several simple hands and a well-designed seat may execute an operation that would otherwise require a complex wrist. This retains the cooperative character of the machine while exploiting the freedom to design the things it assembles.

The meaningful evidence is the completed insertion or release distribution across known starting errors, not just a nominal CAD fit. [Release and retention](../03-physics/release-and-retention.md) gives the force and observation context.
