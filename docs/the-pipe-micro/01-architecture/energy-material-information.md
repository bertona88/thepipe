# Energy, material and information paths

The chamber is traversed by several different kinds of connection. Keeping them explicit exposes couplings that a picture of bare arms would hide.

| Path | Outside the working volume | Through the boundary | Inside the volume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical effort | Motors, capstans, accessible tension measurement | Sealed tendon passage or a supported transmission assembly | Tendons, guides, flexures, tool |
| Vacuum | Pump, regulator, valve, pressure measurement | Tubing feedthrough | Flexible service segment and optional printed lumen |
| Liquid | Reservoir, metering and isolation | Fluid feedthrough | Optional capillary or wetting tool |
| Conditioning | Filtration, temperature/RH conditioning | Controlled inlet and outlet | Low-disturbance conditioned working atmosphere |
| Objects and tools | Prepared cassette | Part or tool transfer chamber | Fixture, tool dock, assembly station |
| Geometric information | Cameras, illumination and processing | Optical windows or suitable ports | Tool features, part features and references |

An external valve changes a distal pressure only after the intervening line responds. An external load sensor sees the tendon transmission as well as the tool. A chamber RH probe measures its local gas, not necessarily the moisture state of a jaw contact. These distinctions are why the machine needs local observation and empirical transmission characterisation.

## Boundary choices

The goal is a closed, serviceable chamber. Tendon feedthroughs can create friction and particles; moving tubing can apply restoring forces; windows can alter image formation. A sealed internal tendon drive stage with external power transfer is another possible arrangement, but has different complexity. The atlas does not select a feedthrough technology.

Keep flow and particle-generating mechanisms away from the immediate assembly site where practical. A large optical enclosure can provide space for baffles, service loops and sacrificial surfaces. The low mass of a part makes gas disturbances relevant even if the distal robot is light.

## No hidden motion source

Pressure can deform a hollow arm. Tendon preload can pull a carriage off its nominal path. A flexure can move as its polymer creeps. A surface charge can change a part's approach before visible contact. The model should name these effects at the fidelity needed by the operation, as discussed in [measurement and models](../05-evidence/measurement-and-models.md).
