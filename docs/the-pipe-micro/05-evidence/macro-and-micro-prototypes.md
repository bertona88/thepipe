# Macro and micro prototypes

## Macro demonstrator

The user expects the prototype to be 3D printed at macroscale. An enlarged flexure-arm demonstrator can preserve the functional arrangement of shoulder, compliant joints, tendons, gripper and cooperating receiver. FDM, SLA or MSLA can be considered according to the feature and material needs.

A roughly 5–10× enlargement was discussed. This does not require every length to scale uniformly. A micro flexure's strain or stiffness may require a different printable macro section, and those departures should be visible in the description.

Useful macro observations include whether tendons can be installed, whether they cross or rub, how base travel changes routing, whether arms can reach a handoff posture, how tool jaws open, and whether camera placement can support the intended sequence.

Macro prototypes can also reveal control and collision mistakes. They cannot establish that a microtexture releases a 300 µm part or that a native-resin surface has acceptable electrostatic behaviour.

## Micro specimens

The small-scale counterpart can be a manageable specimen rather than a complete cell: a flexure, a tendon anchor, a gripper, a channel segment, a contact pad and representative parts. The specimen should retain the features that govern the question being asked.

For example, a rigid pad can compare contact geometries but does not prove the full jaw will apply the same preload. A channel in a rigid block can demonstrate cleaning and flow but does not establish behaviour when bent through a flexure.

## What transfers between scales

| Feature | Can be shared conceptually | Requires scale-specific evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arm/jaw topology | Joint relationships and intended motion | Real stiffness, strain and fatigue |
| Tendon route | Path and assembly accessibility | Friction, fibre damage, stretch and preload |
| Handoff sequence | Support and observation logic | Actual retention and release forces |
| Contact texture | Pattern family and release direction | Deformation, adhesion, wetting and wear |
| Chamber concept | Transfer access and conditioning roles | Flow disturbance, charge and moisture response |
| Optical features | Marker identity and placement intent | Resolvability and calibrated accuracy |

An enlarged model is a useful explanation of the topology. A rendered animation can later show its executed behaviour. Neither should be presented as microscale validation without the corresponding small-scale evidence.
