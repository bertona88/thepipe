# Candidate transport mechanisms

These are alternatives, not components to combine by default.

| Candidate | How it provides motion | Appeal | Main unresolved issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Translating support ring with circumferential carriages | Ring translates in z; bases move around the ring | Natural separation of coordinates | Arms on one ring share z; rings and supports can obstruct views |
| Cylindrical guide with polymer shoes and cable drive | Guidance constrains a shoe; cables supply tangential/axial effort | Lightweight, accessible fabrication | Complete constraint, stick-slip, wear and cable routing |
| Nested rotary and linear stages | Two mechanically distinct stages | Clear kinematics and ordinary construction methods | Bulk, seals and moving service bundles |
| Cable-supported carrier | Tension network positions and stabilises the base | Potentially open geometry | Requires enough independent constraints, positive tension and adequate stiffness |
| Small-range flexure support | Compliant stage provides local z and θ-like travel | Clean, backlash-free elastic guidance in its operating region | Elastic travel is finite; cannot supply unrestricted circulation |
| Several station supports with limited repositioning | Local bases work at selected positions | Simple first physical embodiment | Reduced mobility compared with the full cylindrical concept |

## What selects among them

The relevant comparison is the useful set of shoulder poses under load, while allowing the cameras and other arms to work. A low nominal positioning error has little value if the support blocks the receiving view or if the tendon bundle makes the stated travel impossible.

Independent motion is also a design choice. A common ring may be entirely adequate for some operations, while independent z motion could be valuable for tool exchange or handoff. Calling both arrangements “z + θ” hides an important difference.

## Continuous rotation is optional

Full circulation around the cylinder is appealing. Endless rotation with externally connected tendons and fluid lines adds routing or rotary-interface complexity. Bounded azimuthal travel, service loops and a known unwind posture are legitimate candidates. They should be represented honestly in the available workspace.

The O'Neill-cylinder inspiration survives all these options: a shared interior with access from around its boundary. It does not choose bearings, rails or unlimited rotation for us.
