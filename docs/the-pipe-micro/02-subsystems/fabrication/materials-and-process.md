# Materials and process identity

“Methacrylate plastic” is a family description, not a full material specification. A cured 2PP network can differ from PMMA and from another photopolymer in mechanical response, surface chemistry, moisture response and optical appearance.

## Structural and surface questions

The structural regions need adequate stiffness, recoverable strain and stability under the intended load history. The contact regions need useful friction, adhesion, wear and release behaviour. A single resin can serve both while geometry changes their function, but one favourable property does not imply all the others.

Published PMMA contact angles, charge-decay results or elastic moduli should not be inserted as measurements of an unidentified printed resin. Use them as hypotheses and comparison points.

## A useful specimen identity

| Record | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Resin and batch | Links results to the actual material |
| Printer/process and relevant settings | Geometry and conversion can depend on fabrication |
| Orientation and support strategy | Influences access, local shape and deformation |
| Development, cleaning and post-cure | Defines the surface and completed network presented to the experiment |
| Geometry revision and measured critical dimensions | Separates design intent from the tested specimen |
| Storage and environmental conditioning | Preserves moisture and ageing context |
| Tendon, coating or attached component, if present | Identifies the actual interface and load path |

This is enough to keep findings interpretable without turning material qualification into a separate grand project.

## Shrinkage in proportion

The user considers shrinkage manageable. This atlas treats it as an ordinary fabrication and calibration parameter, not a reason to defer the concept. Where a channel closes, a hinge thickness changes or a mating feature no longer fits, actual geometry still matters. Compensation and measurement belong close to the affected function.

## Mechanical evidence

Elastic response, creep, recovery, hysteresis and cycle life should be discussed for the relevant strain, dwell time, environment and fabrication condition. There is no universal allowable flexure angle derived from the word “methacrylate.”

## Surface evidence

Native smooth, sparse-contact and directional specimens should share the same controlled process where possible. Any later surface treatment adds a new specimen condition. A coating can alter the microfeature radius and compliance as well as its chemistry, so it cannot always be interpreted as a purely chemical change.
