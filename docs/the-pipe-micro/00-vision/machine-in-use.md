# The machine in use

This is an illustrative operation that connects the concept's pieces. It is not a report of an executed experiment.

## Arrival

A small cassette carries several approximately 500 µm components and a receiving assembly fixture. It enters a transfer chamber. After the compartment is closed and its conditions are suitable, the cassette is moved into an observed working region. A fixture presents components in known, accessible orientations rather than leaving loose parts to behave like a macroscale pile.

The cameras inspect the part and its surroundings. A transparent part may carry designed relief features outside the functional contact surfaces. The optical system estimates pose and confidence. The machine also knows which tools are installed and whether the environment is settled.

## Acquisition

Arm A approaches using a compliant mechanical fingertip. Its contact pads touch defined handling surfaces. Closing the gripper establishes a holding interface. A small, bounded motion tests whether the object follows the intended tool relationship. Apparent contact alone is insufficient evidence of a dependable grasp.

The arm transports the component through a visible route. The base provides travel toward the task region; the distal arm supplies local adjustment. Tendons and any hoses have their own clearance and service constraints.

## Cooperative orientation

Arm B contacts a second handling region. A receiver-retention check supports gradual unloading of Arm A. Arm A changes its contact geometry, perhaps opening one jaw first and peeling away. The cameras inspect relative motion and the separation gap. If a connection persists, the operation remains a transfer in progress.

Arm B can now present a face that Arm A could not expose with its original grasp. A nearby fixture or another arm may provide the next temporary support. Cooperative regrasp is how the system earns useful orientation freedom without assuming every arm has a full six-axis wrist.

## Placement

The receiver brings the component to a printed seat. Lead-ins and compliant locating features guide small residual errors. The approach remains guarded because a flexure can also store enough energy to damage a small component.

The destination supplies retention appropriate to the task: a geometric capture, a second arm, or a qualified contact interface. The tool gradually unloads, opens or vents, then withdraws along a designed release direction. Inspection checks both the component's final pose and its separation from the departing tool.

## Tool change and environmental recovery

A tool that accumulates residue can be parked for inspection or replaced with a clean cartridge. A humidity-conditioning experiment occurs with parts retained and with sufficient time for the relevant surfaces to settle. The machine resumes precision manipulation only under conditions compatible with the calibrated operation.
