# Contact, adhesion and stiction

## Real contact and nominal contact

A jaw can look like it touches a large area while actual intimate contact occurs at a few features. Load, surface geometry and local compliance determine how those contacts grow. Designed texture can create predictable locations, separation and release directions. It can also create a strong adhesive pad when compliant features conform to the object.

The intended mechanism should therefore be stated: reduced contact fraction, directional conformity, geometric constraint, liquid management or a pressure seal. “More textured” does not identify which one is working.

## Dry close-contact adhesion

van der Waals interactions become relevant when surfaces approach closely. Deliberately sparse contacts can keep much of the nominal area separated, while smoother or more conformal regions can support greater intimate contact. Actual pull-off depends on the contact mechanics and surface condition, not just a nominal material constant.

The user's smooth-surface capability is valuable here: a smooth native-resin control can be compared with deliberately structured contacts made from the same material. Smoothness is a useful branch of the design space, not a manufacturing defect.

## Capillary interfaces

A liquid bridge can apply force through surface tension and pressure associated with its curvature. Contact geometry, liquid volume, contact angles and their history determine whether the bridge helps pickup or prevents release. Open grooves can drain liquid in one arrangement and encourage wicking in another.

Humidity-driven moisture and deliberately dispensed liquid are different operating conditions. A successful wet-tool demonstration does not imply the same texture releases well in ambient dry manipulation. A published textured capillary gripper is a useful precedent for changing contact configuration; see [sources](../06-reference/sources-and-glossary.md).

## Electrostatics and contamination

Charge can act before contact and persist after separation. It need not be removed by reducing nominal contact area. Contamination can introduce new conductive paths, insulating films, liquid bridges or asperities. It can thus change several mechanisms at once rather than simply adding an independent “dirt force.”

## Friction and stiction

Tangential holding depends on normal loading, adhesion, material behaviour and geometry. Interlocking ribs can provide a different retention mode from a flat friction pad. Stiction describes an observed resistance to initiating motion or separation; it is not necessarily a unique microscopic force with a universal coefficient.

## A useful first model

Use measured conditional distributions for pull-off, shear retention, slip and release outcome. Condition them on load, approach/separation path, speed, dwell, material pair, environment and history. Mechanistic models can explain trends, but adding several nominal adhesion forces independently can double-count coupled effects.

The [measurement document](../05-evidence/measurement-and-models.md) describes how to preserve this distinction between empirical behaviour and explanatory models.
